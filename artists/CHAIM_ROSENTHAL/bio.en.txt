Chaim Rosenthal (born 1938)

Country of Origin: Romania

Status: Jewish Child in Romania During the Holocaust

Biography

Chaim Rosenthal, also known as Chaim Peter Rosenthal, was born in 1938 in Bucharest, Romania. His early childhood coincided with the years of World War II and the period of antisemitic persecution in Romania.

In 1950, Rosenthal immigrated to Israel. In his new country, he received an artistic education and became part of the generation of artists who shaped the Israeli art scene in the second half of the twentieth century.

He studied at the Bezalel Academy of Arts and Design in Jerusalem and later at the Avni Institute in Tel Aviv. Among his teachers were Dan Hoffner and Abba Fenichel.

The War Years and Immigration to Israel

In 1950, after the war and after the establishment of the State of Israel, Rosenthal arrived in Israel and began a new life connected with art, teaching, and cultural activity.

Artistic Style and Subject Matter

Chaim Rosenthal is known as an Israeli artist who worked in painting, drawing, and graphic art. His works include landscapes, city views, figurative compositions, and scenes of everyday life.

His artistic language combines close observation, expressive line, and attention to color and composition. His work reflects an interest in the Israeli landscape, the urban environment, and the human figure.

Alongside his artistic practice, Rosenthal was also an educator and cultural figure. He taught art, served as dean of the College of Painting and Art in Ramat Gan, lectured at Tel Aviv University, and from 1976 to 1979 served as Israel’s cultural attaché in the United States.

Collections and Exhibitions

Works by Chaim Rosenthal are represented in museum and private collections in Israel. His art has been shown in solo and group exhibitions in Tel Aviv, Herzliya, and other cities.

Today, Rosenthal holds a place among Israeli artists of Romanian origin whose lives connect childhood in wartime Europe, immigration to Israel, and a long-standing contribution to art, education, and cultural diplomacy.
