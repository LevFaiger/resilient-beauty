Short Biography
Jacob Rosenbaum was born in Suwałki, Poland, in 1924. His early life was marked by the trauma of World War II, during which he was imprisoned as a Holocaust survivor in German Concentration Camps.

Following the war, he pursued his artistic passion, studying at The Free Academy of Art in Rome, Italy, from 1948 to 1949. He made Aliyah (immigrated) to Israel in 1949, eventually settling in Givat Hamivtar, Jerusalem.

Rosenbaum was recognized for his contributions to the Israeli art scene, being elected to the Israeli Association of Painters and Sculptors in 1987. His work earned him the Jerusalem Prize for Painting and Sculpture in 1977, where he was also specifically recognized with the Jerusalem Prize for Sculpture in memory of Abraham Arest. His artistic career spanned decades, with his life ending in 2015.

Painting Style and Subject Matter
While a comprehensive, formal art-historical analysis of Jacob Rosenbaum's style is less readily available than for some of his contemporaries, his body of work and auction records point to several key characteristics:

Genre: He is primarily known as a painter, with works also including drawings (such as a portrait of his father, a survivor of the Lukov Ghetto).

Subject: His work frequently included Still Life compositions, particularly oil on canvas pieces from the 1950s that have been described as Vintage or Mid-20th Century in style. He also explored portraiture and other figurative work, such as a piece titled "The Humoristic Character 'Arieh'" and "The Mask and the Truth."

Medium: His known works include Oil on Canvas and Pencil Drawing on Paper.

Contextual Influences: As a European-trained immigrant artist in Israel during the mid-20th century, his work belongs to a generation of artists whose styles often blended the traditions of European Modernism with emerging Israeli art, frequently reflecting the history and character of his new home, Jerusalem. The subject matter of Holocaust survival and human character in his drawings points toward a deeply personal and potentially expressive or narrative layer beneath the more traditional still life compositions.
