🇵🇱 Poland

Jacob Rosenbaum (1924–2015)

Country of Origin: Poland

Status: Holocaust Survivor

Biography

Jacob Rosenbaum was born in January 1924 in Suwałki, Poland, into a family of painters. His father and older brother were painters as well, and they passed the profession on to the youngest member of the family. From childhood he aspired to study painting and become an artist.

War Years and Emigration

When World War II broke out in 1939, the Germans entered his city, dispossessed his entire family of home and property, and deported them to the Łomża ghetto. As a young teenager Rosenbaum experienced the horrors of the Holocaust — in wanderings and desperate escapes along roads and off-road paths, and in forced labor.

With the end of the war, Rosenbaum reached Italy with the help of the Joint (American Jewish Joint Distribution Committee), together with a group of Holocaust survivors. The desire to study painting and become an artist, which had accompanied him since childhood, was reawakened, and he enrolled at the Free Academy of Art in Rome. He immigrated to Israel in 1949, settled in Jerusalem, raised his family there, and painted many of his works in the city.

Life and Career in Israel

Rosenbaum built his career over decades of solo and group exhibitions in Israel and abroad. His work received critical acclaim. The art critic Yona Fischer wrote that Rosenbaum's paintings stand halfway between stylized landscape and constructive abstraction, in the heritage of Cubism. Fischer praised his originality — "he paints as though no one had ever painted this way before" — and his sharp, wakeful color that maintains a clear connection to nature and especially to landscape.

The shadow of the Holocaust appears in many of his works, in associative or symbolic form, expressing inner drives born of deep personal experience. Jerusalem landscapes held a central place in his oeuvre.

Rosenbaum won the Jerusalem Prize for Painting in 1977, a decade after the reunification of the city. He died on January 19, 2015, and was buried on Mount Herzl (Har HaMenuchot) in Jerusalem.

Artistic Style and Subject Matter

Rosenbaum worked primarily as a painter in oil on canvas. His style combines landscape observation with semi-abstract and constructivist tendencies. Critics noted his vivid, energetic palette and his distinctive approach to nature and Jerusalem cityscapes. Holocaust memory runs through much of his work in symbolic and associative imagery rather than direct narrative.

Collections

Works by Jacob Rosenbaum are documented in the authority files of the National Library of Israel, the Getty ULAN, and VIAF. His paintings have been shown at the Israel Museum, Tel Aviv Museum, and other public institutions in Israel and abroad.
