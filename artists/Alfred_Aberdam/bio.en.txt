🎨 Alfred Aberdam (1894–1963) - Painter of the School of Paris
Short Biography
Alfred Aberdam was born in Lviv (Lvov), the capital of Galicia (then part of the Austro-Hungarian Empire, now Ukraine) in 1894. He studied art and became one of the prominent painters of the "School of Paris" (École de Paris), a group of artists, many of whom were Jewish immigrants from Eastern Europe, who worked in Paris at the beginning of the 20th century.

He was actively involved in the artistic scenes in both Poland and France before World War II. He exhibited in the "Poland" exhibition at the Gallery of Modern Art in 1929, and also held exhibitions in Warsaw and Lviv in 1932.

After World War II, Aberdam continued to exhibit his work internationally:

Paris (1952)

London (1961)

Tel Aviv (1962)

Alfred Aberdam lived and worked primarily in Paris, where he also passed away in 1963.

Painting Style and Subject Matter
As a representative painter of the "School of Paris," Alfred Aberdam's style integrated the modernistic trends of the period while maintaining a distinctive personal voice:

🖌️ Style and Technique
School of Paris: His style belongs to the expressive-figurative movement characteristic of many artists in the School of Paris. These artists embraced the influences of late Impressionism and Post-Impressionism, often with touches of Expressionism.

Strong Coloration: Aberdam is typically known for his use of rich, warm, and pronounced coloration, often employing a palette that combines dark and bright tones to create a dramatic atmosphere.

Expressiveness: His paintings tend to feature expressive and free brushstrokes, lending the work a sense of movement and emotion.

🖼️ Subject Matter
Portraits and Figures: Like many members of the School of Paris, he focused extensively on portraits and figures, capturing emotional states and emphasizing facial features.

Still Life: He also created still life paintings, often utilizing everyday objects to convey a sense of intimacy or a particular mood.

Urban Landscapes: As an artist living in Paris, the City of Lights, it is likely he painted urban landscapes, though the themes of portraiture and still life generally stand out more prominently in art records.
