🇨🇿 Czechoslovakia

Yehuda Bacon (1929–2022)

Country of Origin: Czechoslovakia

Status: Survivor of Theresienstadt, Auschwitz, and the Death March

Biography

Yehuda Bacon was born in 1929 in Ostrava, Czechoslovakia, into a Jewish family. From an early age he showed a strong interest in drawing and art. His childhood was interrupted by the Nazi occupation, which dramatically changed the course of his life.

Following the war, Bacon became one of Israel’s most distinguished artists, educators, and Holocaust witnesses. His work, deeply rooted in personal experience, has had a profound influence on Israeli art and Holocaust remembrance.

Theresienstadt, Auschwitz, and the Death March

In 1942, at the age of thirteen, Bacon and his family were deported to the Theresienstadt Ghetto. Even under these harsh conditions, he continued to draw and received his first artistic instruction from imprisoned Jewish artists.

In December 1943, Bacon was deported to Auschwitz-Birkenau. In 1944, his father was murdered in the gas chambers. Later, his mother and sister were deported to the Stutthof camp, where they perished shortly before the end of the war.

On January 18, 1945, as Soviet forces approached Auschwitz, Bacon was forced onto one of the infamous Death Marches. Thousands of prisoners marched through snow and freezing temperatures with little food or rest. Many died from starvation, disease, exposure, or execution.

Bacon survived the Death March and was eventually transferred to the Gunskirchen camp, part of the Mauthausen system. On May 5, 1945, he was liberated by the United States Army. He was the sole survivor of his immediate family.

In 1946, Bacon arrived in Eretz Israel through the Youth Aliyah program and began rebuilding his life.

Artistic Style and Subject Matter

After the war, Bacon studied at the Bezalel Academy of Arts and Design in Jerusalem and later became one of its leading professors.

Although the Holocaust remained an important element of his work, Bacon's art extended beyond testimony. His paintings and drawings explored memory, human dignity, spirituality, moral responsibility, and reconciliation.

His Auschwitz drawings later served as evidence during the Eichmann Trial in Jerusalem and the Frankfurt Auschwitz Trials.

Collections

Works by Yehuda Bacon are held in major collections worldwide, including Yad Vashem, the Israel Museum in Jerusalem, the British Museum, the Victoria and Albert Museum, and the Imperial War Museum in London.
