Michel Adlen (1898–1980)

Country of Origin: Ukraine (Russian Empire)

Status: Jewish Artist Who Survived Nazi-Occupied France

Biography

Michel Adlen (born Michel Adler) was born in 1898 in the Russian Empire, in what is now Ukraine. He received his artistic education in Vienna, where he studied from 1915 to 1922 and began exhibiting his works. He later lived and worked in Berlin before settling in Paris in 1923, a city that became the center of his artistic career.

During the interwar years, Adlen gained recognition as both a painter and illustrator. Between 1929 and 1939, he collaborated with leading French newspapers and magazines, participated actively in the artistic life of Paris, and became associated with the renowned École de Paris (School of Paris). He was also among the founders of the Association of Jewish Painters and Sculptors in Paris.

Years of Nazi Occupation

Following the German occupation of France in 1940, Adlen’s life changed dramatically. As a Jewish artist and a prominent figure within the Parisian art world, he faced the threat of arrest and deportation.

During the war, he was forced to live in hiding in France. His artistic activity was largely interrupted, and the occupation years became a period of constant danger and struggle for survival. Unlike many Jewish artists in Paris who perished during the Holocaust, Adlen survived the war.

After the liberation of France, he returned to his Paris studio and resumed painting. The traumatic experiences of the war years left a profound impact on both his worldview and artistic expression.

Artistic Style and Subject Matter

Michel Adlen was one of the notable representatives of the École de Paris, the international community of artists working in France during the first half of the twentieth century. His early works were influenced by Cubism and Fauvism, though he gradually developed a distinctive personal style.

He became especially known for his Parisian cityscapes, suburban landscapes, still lifes, and portraits. After World War II, his paintings often reflected a more melancholic mood, characterized by restrained color palettes and an increased use of gray and muted tones.

His works are distinguished by their atmospheric quality, expressive compositions, and ability to convey not only the appearance of places and objects but also their emotional memory. His art combines lyricism, painterly freedom, and the defining characteristics of the School of Paris.

Collections

Works by Michel Adlen are represented in museum and private collections in France, Israel, Ukraine, and other countries. His paintings continue to be exhibited in exhibitions dedicated to the School of Paris and to Jewish art of the twentieth century.

Today, Michel Adlen is regarded as one of the important Jewish artists of the School of Paris, whose life and work form part of both European art history and the history of the Jewish people in the twentieth century.
