🇵🇱 Poland

Jacob Vassover (1927–2009)

Country of Origin: Poland

Status: Survivor of the Łódź Ghetto, Auschwitz, and the Death March

Biography

Jacob Vassover was born in 1927 in Łódź, one of the largest centers of Jewish life in prewar Poland. From an early age, he showed a talent for drawing and art. His childhood unfolded within the rich cultural world of Polish Jewry, a world that was almost entirely destroyed during the Holocaust.

After the war, Vassover devoted his life to art and to preserving the memory of the vanished Jewish communities of Eastern Europe. His work became a visual memorial to the Jewish world that had been lost.

The Łódź Ghetto, Auschwitz, and the Death March

Following the Nazi occupation of Poland, Jacob Vassover, together with his parents and brother, was forced into the Łódź Ghetto. For nearly five years he lived under conditions of hunger, disease, and constant fear. Despite these circumstances, he continued to draw and created hundreds of sketches documenting life in the ghetto. None of these works survived.

In September 1944, the family was deported from the Łódź Ghetto to Auschwitz-Birkenau. His parents and brother were murdered, while Jacob was selected for forced labor.

In January 1945, as Soviet forces approached Auschwitz, the Nazis evacuated the camp. Vassover was among the prisoners forced onto one of the infamous Death Marches. Thousands perished from starvation, cold, disease, or execution along the route. Vassover survived the march and lived to see liberation.

Of the approximately 250,000 Jews who had lived in Łódź before the war, fewer than 10,000 survived. Vassover was among them.

Artistic Style and Subject Matter

Memory became the central theme of Jacob Vassover’s art. Through his paintings he reconstructed the lost world of Eastern European Jewish life—synagogues, marketplaces, musicians, rabbis, family celebrations, and scenes of everyday life.

His works are characterized by vibrant colors, emotional intensity, and a profound sense of nostalgia. Many of his paintings serve as visual testimonies to communities and traditions that disappeared during the Holocaust.

Collections

Jacob Vassover’s works have been exhibited in Israel, Europe, and North America and are held in numerous private collections. His paintings are valued both as works of art and as important memorials to the destroyed Jewish world of Eastern Europe.
