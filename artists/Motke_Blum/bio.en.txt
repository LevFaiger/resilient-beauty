Mordechai (Motke) Blum (1925–2024)

Country of Origin: Romania

Status: Holocaust Survivor; Illegal Immigration (Ha'apala); Israel Defense Forces Veteran

Biography

Mordechai (Motke) Blum was born on March 4, 1925, in the village of Răcăciuni in Moldova, Romania, to Leah and Aryeh Leib HaCohen. His father worked in a textile factory and the family lived in poverty. When he was five, the family moved to Bucharest. From childhood he dreamed of becoming an artist.

War Years and Immigration

During World War II, Blum spent three years in forced labor camps before managing to escape and immigrate to Eretz Israel in 1944. He was miraculously saved from the sinking of the illegal immigrant ship Mafkura, which was intercepted by a German vessel: he was taken off the ship at the last moment and transferred to another vessel, but lost many of his companions in the disaster.

Life in Israel

His first years in the country were spent at Kibbutz Avuka of the Gordonia movement in the Beit She'an Valley. After contracting malaria in 1946, he moved to Netanya, worked as a plasterer, and joined the Haganah. In 1948 he was drafted into the IDF and fought in the War of Independence. In 1952 he moved to Jerusalem, where he met his wife Shoshana; they married in the early 1950s and had a daughter and a son.

In 1956 he graduated with honors from the Bezalel Academy of Arts and Design, where he studied under the painter Mordecai Ardon and the sculptor Zeev Ben-Zvi. He also trained with Professor Teodoro Orselli, director of the Academy of Fine Arts in Ravenna, Italy, in the restoration and creation of mosaics and frescoes, and continued his studies at the Royal Workshop in Zeist, the Netherlands.

In 1958 he returned to Israel and established a studio in the basement of Talita Kumi House in Jerusalem. In 1964 he embarked on an exhibition tour across Europe, and from 1965 to 1967 lived in the United States, where he exhibited and studied. After returning to Jerusalem, he opened a studio in 1969 in the Hutzot Hayotzer artists' quarter. In 1981 he traveled again to North America on a fellowship to study museums across the United States and Canada.

For forty years Blum worked on the restoration of mosaics and frescoes throughout Israel on behalf of the Israel Antiquities Authority and UNESCO. Two of his Jerusalem paintings were chosen as the basis for posters issued by the Israeli Ministry of Tourism. He also designed posters for the annual Jerusalem Arts Fair, and his works were selected as covers for books published in the United States, Sweden, and Germany. In 1990 the British edition Jerusalem: Reflection of Eternity was published, presenting a dialogue between his paintings and the anthropological writing of Eliane Wilson. His works adorn several synagogues in the United States and a church in Miami. In 1994 Czech Television produced a documentary about him titled All Colors White.

Throughout his life, Blum also used his art for social engagement. During the Six-Day War he volunteered to occupy children from the border neighborhood of Musrara in shelters with communal painting. For many years he volunteered with Akim, conducting art workshops for special populations in Jerusalem. In 2006 he received the Mordechai Ish-Shalom Lifetime Achievement Award, and in 2014 he was named a Yakir Yerushalayim (Distinguished Citizen of Jerusalem) for "glorifying the name and beauty of the city in his work and for his contribution through art to vulnerable populations."

Blum lived in the Rehavia neighborhood of Jerusalem. He died on July 11, 2024, at the age of 99. He is survived by his wife Shoshana and a son; his daughter Anat died in 2018.

Artistic Style and Subject Matter

Motke Blum was a multidisciplinary Israeli artist whose work encompassed painting, drawing, sculpture, mosaics, wall hangings, silver jewelry, and metalwork. He is especially associated with Jerusalem — its landscapes, light, and spiritual atmosphere — rendered in oils, gouache, and mixed media, often with distinctive palette-knife texture. His work is displayed in public institutions in Israel and the United States, in museums, and in prominent private collections.

Collections and Honors

Works by Motke Blum are documented at the Israel Museum Information Center for Israeli Art in Jerusalem. He received the Mordechai Ish-Shalom Lifetime Achievement Award (2006) and Yakir Yerushalayim (2014).
