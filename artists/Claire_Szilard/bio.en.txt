Claire (Klára) Szilard (1921–2017)

Country of Origin: Hungary

Status: Holocaust Refugee

Biography

Claire Szilard (Klára Szilárd) was born on March 31, 1921, in Budapest, Hungary, the daughter of Johanna (née Grota). She grew up in the Hungarian capital and studied at the Free Academy in Budapest from 1940 to 1944.

War Years and Emigration

At the end of World War II she was forced to flee Hungary and found refuge in Switzerland. In 1945 she studied at the School of Fine Arts in Geneva. In 1951 she immigrated to Israel, traveling via Switzerland and France.

Life and Career in Israel

In 1952 she trained in fresco painting at the Academy of Fine Arts in Ravenna, Italy. She was accepted into the Israel Painters and Sculptors Association in 1953 and held her first solo exhibition in Israel in 1954 at the Gordon Gallery in Tel Aviv. In 1967 she moved to Old Jaffa with her first husband, Imre Baum (d. 1971), a French horn player in the Israel Philharmonic Orchestra. Between 1965 and 1973 she designed Israeli Art. In the early 1970s she met her second husband, the artist Peter Rozje. In 1974 she settled in the artists' village of Ein Hod.

Artistic Style and Subject Matter

Szilard worked primarily in painting and stained glass. Her landscape paintings are characterized by rich impasto. The stained glass windows she created, often with her husband, deal with symbolic and biblical themes. She designed stained glass for fourteen synagogues in Israel, including congregations in Tel Aviv–Jaffa, Ramat Gan, Bnei Brak, Sderot, Pardes Hanna, and elsewhere. She also created stage sets for theater productions and wall hangings. From the mid-1980s onward she also produced welded, abstract iron sculptures.

Her famous stained glass memorial in the courtyard of the Great Synagogue of Budapest, dedicated to Raoul Wallenberg, depicts flames from which a serpent emerges — expressing the fire that consumed Jews in the death camps, with the serpent symbolizing the evil that brought about the Holocaust. Inaugurated in 2004 in the Wallenberg Memorial Garden, it stands among her most significant public works.

International Career and Later Life

In 1980 she participated in the Autumn Salon at the Grand Palais in Paris. From 1991 she also worked in Paris, and in 1993 she returned permanently to Budapest, where she lived until her death on September 10, 2017, at the age of ninety-six. A memorial plaque in her honor was unveiled on Dembinszky Street in Budapest in April 2024.

Collections

Works by Claire Szilard are documented in the Information Center for Israeli Art at the Israel Museum, Jerusalem, and in the Museum of Fine Arts, Budapest. Her stained glass and paintings are installed in synagogues and public institutions in Israel, Hungary, and abroad.
