🇵🇱 Poland (Rava-Ruska, Galicia)

David Lan-Bar (1911–1987)

Country of Origin: Poland (Rava-Ruska, Galicia)

Status: Jewish Emigrant from Poland

Biography

David Lan-Bar (born David Langberg) was born in 1911 in Rava-Ruska, then part of the Austro-Hungarian Empire and later Poland. From an early age, he showed a strong interest in art and dedicated himself to painting.

In 1934, he emigrated from Poland to Jerusalem, where he continued his artistic education and creative development. Among his teachers was the artist Miron Sima. Later, Lan-Bar lived and worked in Tel Aviv, becoming part of the developing artistic community of Mandatory Palestine and the future State of Israel.

In 1948, the artist moved to Paris, where a significant part of his career unfolded. There he became associated with the Second School of Paris and gained international recognition.

Emigration and Life Between Israel and France

Lan-Bar’s move to Eretz Israel in 1934 marked a turning point in his life. In Jerusalem and Tel Aviv, he developed as an independent artist and encountered artistic movements that would influence his future work.

Following the establishment of the State of Israel, Lan-Bar relocated to France. Paris became his second home and the center of his artistic activity. For decades, he maintained close ties with both the Israeli and European art worlds.

Artistic Style and Subject Matter

Lan-Bar’s early works were primarily figurative and expressionist. He painted portraits, still lifes, and landscapes, placing particular emphasis on emotional expression.

Beginning in the 1950s, he gradually turned toward abstraction. His mature style combined elements of Abstract Expressionism and lyrical abstraction. His paintings are characterized by rich color, energetic brushwork, and a complex painterly surface.

Through the interaction of color and form, Lan-Bar sought to convey movement, light, and emotion. His work occupies an important place among Jewish artists associated with both Paris and Israel during the second half of the twentieth century.

Collections

Works by David Lan-Bar are represented in major museum and private collections worldwide, including the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) in New York, the Art Institute of Chicago, the Smithsonian American Art Museum, and the Musée National d’Art Moderne in Paris.

His works are also held in numerous private collections throughout Europe, Israel, and the United States.
