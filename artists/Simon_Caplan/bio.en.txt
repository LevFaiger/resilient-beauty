Simon Caplan (1924–) - The Portrait Painter of Rishpon
Short Biography
Simon Caplan was born in Poland in 1924. His family moved to France when he was one year old, and he began studying classical painting in Paris at age 12. Following the German invasion in May 1940, his family fled to southern France, and by the end of 1941, they sailed from Portugal to Brazil.

In Rio de Janeiro, Simon continued his studies at the Academy of Art. There, he met and married Paula (née Lobicz) in 1947. They were members of the Jewish youth movement "Dror" and made Aliyah to Israel in 1948 with the first group of pioneers from South America, later becoming founders of Kibbutz Bror Hayil in 1952.

Frustrated by his inability to dedicate time to painting while working as a dairy farmer, the Caplans left the kibbutz in 1956 and purchased a farm in Rishpon, hoping to combine agriculture and art. By 1960, Simon transitioned to a full-time role as a painter (designated as a "public worker" by the Moshav). He built a uniquely designed home in Rishpon tailored to his profession, becoming known as "The Painter of the Moshav."

Painting Style and Subject Matter
Simon Caplan devoted his life to painting, teaching, and community involvement, with the portrait being the central focus of his work.

🖌️ Style and Technique
Classical Foundation: Caplan began his training with classical painting studies in Paris at a young age, which informed the technical fundamentals of his work.

Technical Evolution:

Early Period (Up to 1980): The majority of his work focused on charcoal drawings. Charcoal requires precision and technical skill and is effective at capturing facial expression with sharp clarity.

Later Period (From 1980 onwards): He transitioned to painting mainly in oil colors. This shift allowed for a richer color palette, depth, and texture in his compositions.

🖼️ Subject Matter
Portraiture: This is the most significant and defining subject of his work. He was known as "The Painter of the Moshav" and painted portraits of many Rishpon residents, focusing particularly on children and adults alike. Portrait painting requires high technical ability and psychological sensitivity to capture the uniqueness of the sitter.

Teaching and Outreach: In addition to his personal practice, he dedicated decades to teaching painting in his home studio in Rishpon, passing on his classical knowledge to generations of students from the Moshav and surrounding communities.

Community Art: Simon Caplan actively utilized his talent for the community, illustrating stage backdrops for shows and plays in the Moshav, and happily created various illustrations for special requests from residents.
