🇵🇱 Poland

Simon Caplan (1924–2017)

Country of Origin: Poland

Status: Jewish Wartime Refugee

Biography

Simon Caplan was born in Poland in 1924. As a child, he moved with his family to France, where he spent his formative years. From an early age, he showed a strong interest in art, which would later become his life's vocation.

After World War II, Caplan studied at the Academy of Fine Arts in Rio de Janeiro. While in Brazil, he met Paula Levitz, who would become his wife and lifelong companion.

Following the establishment of the State of Israel, the couple decided to build their future in the Jewish homeland. As members of the Dror movement, they underwent agricultural training in preparation for immigration and arrived in Israel in the early 1950s. After living and working in agricultural settlements in southern Israel, they purchased a farm in the moshav of Rishpon in 1956.

After several years of farming, Caplan returned to his artistic calling. With the encouragement of the Moshav Movement, he built his own studio in Rishpon and devoted decades to painting and teaching art.

War Years and Emigration

In May 1940, following the German invasion of France, the Caplan family was forced to flee south. After a period of displacement, they reached Marseille and, at the end of 1941, were able to leave Europe through Lisbon, Portugal, sailing to Brazil together with thousands of other refugees.

The move to South America marked a new chapter in Caplan's life. In Brazil, he completed his artistic education and began developing as a professional painter.

Artistic Style and Subject Matter

Simon Caplan's work is closely connected to the landscapes of Israel, rural life, Jerusalem, and Jewish culture. His paintings are characterized by warmth, sensitivity, and a deep appreciation for everyday life.

A central theme in his work is the depiction of Jerusalem, Israeli landscapes, and scenes from the life of the young state. His paintings combine careful observation with a personal and emotional response to the world around him.

Collections

Works by Simon Caplan are represented in the collection of the Israel Museum in Jerusalem, as well as in public and private collections in Israel and abroad.

Family

In 1947, Simon Caplan married Paula Levitz. Together they made the journey from Brazil to Israel, took part in the development of the young state, and spent many years living and working in Rishpon, where the artist created much of his artistic legacy.
