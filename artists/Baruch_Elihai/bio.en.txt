🇩🇪 Germany

Baruch Elihai (1923–)

Country of Origin: Germany

Status: Kindertransport Refugee; Holocaust Survivor

Biography

Baruch Elihai was born in October 1923 in Frankfurt am Main, Germany, as Baruch-Beno Schaechter, the son of Yechiel (Ichel) Schaechter and Sara (née Weinreb). He grew up in an Orthodox Jewish family. From an early age he showed an interest in art that would shape his later life.

Nazi Years and Emigration

In August 1939, shortly before the outbreak of World War II, Baruch was sent to England as part of the Kindertransport rescue operation. He lived in London with a foster family and studied art there. His parents and his older sister Eva remained in Germany, where they found hiding places, but were eventually discovered. After various ordeals they were murdered in Auschwitz. His sisters Heidy and Mimi survived the war and later reunited with their brother.

In 1948, following the establishment of the State of Israel, Baruch immigrated to the country and adopted the surname Elihai. He studied at the Bezalel Academy of Arts and Design in Jerusalem. After marrying, he settled in Haifa, where he taught painting and exhibited his work in various venues. For many years he maintained a studio on Dov Pinsker Street in Haifa.

Artistic Style and Subject Matter

Baruch Elihai is known primarily as a painter of Israeli landscapes and still lifes. His work includes oils on canvas and masonite, as well as watercolors. His paintings are typically signed and dated. Subjects include valley landscapes, still lifes with lamps, and scenes from the Israeli environment, rendered in a direct, observational manner with attention to light and color.

Collections

Works by Baruch Elihai are documented in the Information Center for Israeli Art at the Israel Museum, Jerusalem. Personal papers, letters, and photographs relating to him and his family are preserved in the archives of the Ghetto Fighters' House (file no. 40569).
