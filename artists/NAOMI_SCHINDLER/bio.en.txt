🇩🇪 Germany

Naomi Schindler (1919–1991)

Country of Origin: Germany

Status: Jewish Refugee from Nazi Germany

Biography

Naomi Schindler was born in Berlin in 1919. She grew up in a Jewish family and received her education in Jewish schools in the city. From an early age, she developed an interest in art that would later shape her life and career.

In 1936, Schindler immigrated to Eretz Israel. Until 1965, she was a member of a kibbutz, and later became a recognized Israeli sculptor. She was a member of the Israel Association of Painters and Sculptors and participated in exhibitions beginning in 1943.

Nazi Years and Emigration

Like many Jewish children in Berlin at the time, Naomi began her studies in a state Jewish school and later attended the Karl Marx Secondary School, where many Jewish students studied before the rise of the Nazi regime.

After the Nazis came to power in 1933, Jewish students were forced to leave the school. Naomi was transferred to a Jewish boarding school near Berlin, where education was strongly influenced by the Zionist ideas of the philosopher Martin Buber. This period had a profound impact on her worldview and Jewish identity.

In 1936, amid the growing persecution of Jews in Germany, she left her homeland and arrived in Eretz Israel, where she began a new chapter in her life.

Artistic Style and Subject Matter

Naomi Schindler was known primarily as a sculptor. Throughout her life, she continued to explore new materials and artistic techniques, seeking to unite form, movement, and space. Her works are characterized by harmony, clarity of line, and a subtle combination of realistic and abstract elements.

Her sculptures often reflect a search for inner balance, spiritual growth, and harmony. They are distinguished by refined forms and a particular sensitivity to the relationship between volume and space.

Collections

Naomi Schindler was a member of the Israel Association of Painters and Sculptors and participated in numerous exhibitions beginning in 1943. Her works are represented in museum and private collections throughout Israel.
