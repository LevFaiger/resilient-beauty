Emanuel Bar-Kadma (1932–2018)

Country of Origin: Romania

Status: Jewish Refugee from Pro-Nazi Romania

Biography

Emanuel Bar-Kadma (born Emanuel Mizrahi) was born in Bucharest, Romania, in 1932 into a Jewish family. His father was a lawyer and an active supporter of the Zionist movement.

In 1941, amid growing antisemitic persecution and the pro-Nazi policies of the Ion Antonescu regime, the family immigrated to Eretz Israel. There, Bar-Kadma spent his childhood and youth, becoming part of the generation that helped shape the cultural life of the future State of Israel.

After completing his education, he lived for a period in Kibbutz Netiv HaLamed-Heh and later pursued both artistic and journalistic careers.

Escape from Romania and a New Life in Eretz Israel

When the family left Romania in 1941, the situation of Romanian Jewry was rapidly deteriorating. The Antonescu regime collaborated with Nazi Germany and implemented harsh anti-Jewish policies.

Arriving in Eretz Israel as a child, Bar-Kadma escaped the catastrophe that would soon engulf many Jewish communities of Eastern Europe. His father was forced to abandon his legal career and begin a new life from scratch.

The experience of immigration and adaptation to a new homeland became an important part of Bar-Kadma’s personal story and worldview.

Artistic Style and Subject Matter

Bar-Kadma was known primarily as a draftsman and graphic artist. The human figure, portraiture, and the female form occupied a central place in his work.

His drawings are characterized by confident, expressive lines and a remarkable ability to capture personality and emotion with great economy of means.

Alongside his artistic work, Bar-Kadma became a prominent figure in Israeli journalism. For more than forty years he worked for Yedioth Ahronoth as an editor, theater critic, film critic, and one of the founders of the influential cultural supplement 7 Nights.

Collections

Works by Emanuel Bar-Kadma are represented in museum and private collections in Israel. His art has been exhibited in Tel Aviv, Jerusalem, Haifa, Paris, and other cities.
