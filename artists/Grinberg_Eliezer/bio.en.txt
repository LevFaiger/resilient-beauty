Eliezer Greenberg: Biographical and Artistic Summary
Eliezer Greenberg was an Israeli painter born in Lviv, Poland (now Ukraine). His personal life and artistic output were shaped by early training, the upheaval of World War II, and his immigration to Israel.

🖼️ Early Training and WWII Period
Education: His talent was recognized early by his uncle, an architect and painter. He studied at the School of Decorative Arts in Lviv (graduating in 1931) and was accepted to the Academy of Art in Kraków.

Captivity and Art: When World War II broke out in 1939, Greenberg was drafted into the Polish Army, captured by the Russians, and transferred to Kryvyi Rih, Ukraine. He secured his release by successfully painting a portrait of the composer Tchaikovsky, who was celebrating his 100th birthday.

In the USSR: He worked as a decorator for the local opera house in Frunze (now Bishkek), Kyrgyzstan. He was accepted into the Russian Union of Artists and participated in general exhibitions across the Soviet Union. He met his wife, Eugenia-Gila, there.

🇮🇱 Immigration and Life in Israel
Displaced Persons (She'erit HaPletah): In 1945, he returned to Poland, only to find that just one of his sisters had survived the Holocaust. They moved to a Displaced Persons camp near Ulm, Germany, where he participated in exhibitions of the "She'erit HaPletah" (Surviving Remnant) artists' association.

Aid Work: During his time in Germany, Greenberg helped locate orphaned child survivors of the Holocaust and arrange for their transfer to Israel.

Aliyah: He immigrated to Israel in June 1949 aboard the ship Negba. The family initially lived in a hut in the Bat Galim transit camp (Ma'abara) in Haifa. He suffered a serious illness and was hospitalized for several months.

Later Life: In 1952, the family moved to a small apartment in a housing project for new immigrants in Kiryat Motzkin, where he lived until his death from a heart ailment in 1965.

🎨 Style and Work
Stylistic Shift: Accustomed to the conservative, dark, and gray realist-naturalist style of Russian painting, Greenberg was captivated by the Israeli sun and landscape. His style dramatically changed to Impressionism, full of light and color.

Influences: In his later years, many of his works show the strong influence of the Expressionist style, particularly the Dutch painter Van Gogh, whom he admired.

Subject Matter: During his years in Israel, Greenberg frequently traveled and painted Israeli landscapes, including Haifa, Tiberias, Safed, Acre, Jerusalem, Tel Aviv-Jaffa, and Netanya. He also painted portraits (including self-portraits and portraits of his daughters) and subjects related to Judaism and the Holocaust.

Recognition: He was a member of the Israeli Painters Association, exhibiting in many group and solo shows both in Israel and Europe (Brussels, Luxembourg). His works were acquired by private collectors and public institutions, including the Presidential Residence (Ben-Zvi), the Ghetto Fighters' Museum, and the Haifa Museum.
