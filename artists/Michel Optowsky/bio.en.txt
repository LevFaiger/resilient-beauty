Graphic Designer, Illustrator, Painter, and Avant-Garde Creator

1947 – 2004

English Version
Biography
Michel Optowsky was a pioneering graphic designer, visual artist, cartographer, and illustrator who left an indelible mark on Israeli culture, media, and the underground art scene during the late 20th century.

He was born in 1947 in a displaced persons camp in Sweden to Holocaust survivor parents who had lost everything in Europe. Raised in France, this complex European cultural heritage, combined with the profound experience of being a second-generation survivor, served as a lifelong emotional anchor and a powerful engine for his visual and conceptual creations.

In 1964, Optowsky immigrated to Israel. He received his formal artistic training at the Bezalel Academy of Arts and Design in Jerusalem, graduating with honors from the Graphic Design department in the early 1970s. Upon graduation, he rapidly became a key figure in the Tel Aviv cultural and design scene.

Avant-Garde and Underground Movement: "The Third Eye" and "Pahhad"
In 1971, Optowsky joined the revolutionary avant-garde artist collective "The Third Eye" (Ha'ayin Hashlishit) in Tel Aviv. Led by his close friend, the visionary filmmaker and artist Jacques Mori Katmor, the group introduced psychedelic culture, mysticism, and underground comic aesthetics to Israel. Through this partnership, Optowsky integrated international movements like Dada, Surrealism, and Pop Art into his work. In 1979, he wrote and directed the raw underground musical film Schultz (starring Rami Fortis), which later attained cult status.

During the 1980s, seeking a bold, critical alternative to the mainstream art establishment, Optowsky founded and published "Pahhad" (a Hebrew play on "Fear/Terror"), an independent underground art zine. The publication featured cutting-edge comics, illustration, photography, and social satire blending dark humor, erotica, and psychedelia to reflect the raw existential anxieties of the Israeli experience.

Media Design and Editorial Leadership at "Haaretz"
Parallel to his underground endeavors, Optowsky became a leading designer for major Israeli press. He is widely credited with redefining the visual language, typography, and layout of the prestigious "Haaretz" newspaper (specifically its weekly magazine, Musaf Haaretz), as well as the innovative layouts for Hadashot (including its avant-garde supplement Krua) and Davar.

Cartography and Documentaries: "The White City" and Roadmaps
Optowsky possessed a rare ability to transform complex informational projects into graphic masterpieces. His major achievements in this field include:

"The White City" Map (Bauhaus Architecture): In 1994, for a special project with the Tel Aviv Museum of Art, Optowsky designed the iconic official map charting Tel Aviv's Bauhaus architecture. His visual work greatly contributed to the public mapping and recognition of the heritage that eventually led to Tel Aviv's UNESCO World Heritage designation.

National Roadmaps: He collaborated with premier publishing houses like Mapa to design modern road atlases and commercial cartography, masterfully blending strict utility with exceptional graphic design.

Album Covers and Legacy
Optowsky is celebrated as one of the ultimate visual architects of the Israeli music industry, designing over 150 books and dozens of legendary album covers. From his foundational 1970s designs for Arik Einstein (Se'a Le'at) and Shalom Hanoch (Adam Betoch Atzmo), to his later work for Mashina, Shlomo Artzi, Tato, and Nurit Galron, his art shaped the country's musical landscape.

In 1988, he co-founded the successful agency "Optowsky-Deri". In his later years, he dedicated his time to teaching graphic design. Michel Optowsky passed away in 2004. In 2017, the Herzliya Museum of Contemporary Art honored his legacy with a major retrospective exhibition titled "Michel Optowsky: Pahhad, From the Seventies to Infinity."
