🇩🇪 Germany

Gershon Rennert (1929–2009)

Country of Origin: Germany

Status: Jewish Refugee from Nazi Germany

Biography

Gershon Rennert was born on March 10, 1929, in Mannheim, Germany. After the Nazis rose to power in 1933, his family fled to France and in 1935 immigrated to Eretz Israel. He grew up in Tel Aviv, where he received his education from kindergarten through the completion of his artistic studies.

Nazi Years and Emigration

At the age of four, Rennert left Germany with his family following Hitler's rise to power. After a period in France, the family settled in Palestine in 1935 as part of the Fifth Aliyah. He spent his formative years in the young Jewish community of Tel Aviv.

Life and Career in Israel

Rennert studied painting at the Avni Institute in Tel Aviv and at the Midrasha Art School in Ramat HaSharon (today part of Beit Berl Academic College). Among his teachers were leading figures of Israeli art, including Aharon Avni and Marcel Janco. He became a member of the Israel Painters and Sculptors Association in 1961 and taught painting at Tel Aviv University.

Over the course of his career he held approximately sixty-five solo exhibitions in Israel and Europe and participated in more than two hundred group exhibitions. In 1963 the city of Mannheim purchased one of his works for its public institutions. In 2007 he exhibited in Israel for the first time in fifteen years.

Artistic Style and Subject Matter

Rennert's work draws on diverse sources, including classical painting, surrealism, and fantastic realism, as well as the influence of modern masters such as Picasso. His paintings include surrealist landscapes and figurative compositions rendered in oil on canvas. A documentary film about him, produced as part of the Israeli Archive for Painting and Sculpture project, notes the range of styles present in his oeuvre.

Together with Makabit Eldad, Rennert co-authored the book Exposed (Hebrew: Chashufa). His work is also discussed in Dorit Kedar's doctoral research on Zen Buddhism as a means of enhancing creativity.

Death and Legacy

Rennert died on April 16, 2009, at the age of eighty. He donated his body to science and was later buried at Kiryat Shaul Cemetery in Tel Aviv. He was the father of Iris and Adi Rennert, a musician.

Collections

Works by Gershon Rennert are documented in the Information Center for Israeli Art at the Israel Museum, Jerusalem. His paintings are held in public and private collections in Israel and abroad, including municipal collections in Germany.
