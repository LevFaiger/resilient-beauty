🇩🇪 Germany

Shlomo Van den Berg (1920–1982)

Country of Origin: Germany

Status: Jewish Youth Refugee from Nazi Germany

Biography

Shlomo Van den Berg was born in 1920 in Emden, Germany, to a family of Dutch origin. His parents had moved to Germany from the Netherlands. As a child, he studied at a Jewish elementary school and participated in Zionist youth movements.

After the Nazis came to power, the family’s situation deteriorated sharply. In 1933, as a result of Nazi policy, the family was forced to leave Germany and return to the Netherlands. In 1936, Shlomo Van den Berg arrived in Eretz Israel through Youth Aliyah, a program created to rescue and prepare Jewish youth for life in Eretz Israel.

At first he lived in Kibbutz Tel Yosef, and in 1939 he moved to Kibbutz Maoz Haim, where he worked as a carpenter. Later, in 1941, he worked as a carpenter with a kibbutz movement group at the potash works near the Dead Sea.

Escape from Nazi Germany and Life in Eretz Israel

Shlomo Van den Berg’s story reflects the fate of Jewish teenagers rescued from Europe during the years of Nazi persecution. His arrival in Eretz Israel through Youth Aliyah was part of a wider movement that helped Jewish children and young people leave dangerous environments and begin a new life in Jewish agricultural settlements.

During World War II, Van den Berg served in an engineering unit of the British Army. After the war, he joined the Haganah and served as a camouflage instructor in the engineering corps. This experience of practical labor, construction, and military service became part of his life before his full development as an artist.

After his years in the kibbutzim, he moved to Kfar Vitkin and later to Tel Aviv. In 1953 he went to Paris, where he studied drawing at the Académie de la Grande Chaumière in 1953–1954.

Artistic Style and Subject Matter

Shlomo Van den Berg was a painter whose work covered a wide range of subjects: portraiture, figurative composition, still life, landscape, scenes of labor, and Israeli daily life. His early works are connected with kibbutz life, the Dead Sea, industrial sites, and the image of the human being in a new country.

The Israel Museum archive records works such as Potash Works in Sodom from 1940, Portrait with Hat from 1945, Women from 1955, still lifes, and abstract compositions from the late 1960s. These works show the artist’s transition from observational figurative painting toward a freer and more abstract visual language.

In addition to easel painting, Van den Berg created works for the theater, mosaics, murals, and decorative projects. Among these were theater works, murals in the Ohel Shem hall, and the design of the Israeli pavilion at the Izmir fair.

Exhibitions and Legacy

Shlomo Van den Berg was active in Israeli artistic life. His solo exhibition, Shlomo Van den Berg: Paintings 1967–1968, was held at the Israel Museum in Jerusalem from December 1968 to January 1969.

His works are found in museum archives, private collections, and the Israeli art market. Today, Van den Berg is regarded as a member of the generation of artists whose lives began in Europe under the shadow of Nazi persecution and whose artistic maturity developed in Israel.

His biography connects the experience of a Jewish youth refugee, kibbutz life, military service, European artistic training, and participation in the formation of Israeli visual culture.
