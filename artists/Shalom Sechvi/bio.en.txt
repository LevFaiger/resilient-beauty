🇵🇱 Poland

Shalom Sechvi (1928–2013)

Country of Origin: Poland

Status: Holocaust Survivor and Forced Labor Camp Prisoner

Biography

Shalom Sechvi, born Friedrich Kokotek, was born on May 25, 1928, in Sosnowiec, Poland. He was the son of Abraham and Chaya Kokotek. His childhood and youth coincided with the years of the Nazi occupation of Poland and the Holocaust.

During the war, Sechvi endured camps, forced labor, persecution, and the loss of almost his entire family. At a young age, he was caught in the Nazi camp system and managed to survive conditions that took the lives of most of his loved ones.

After the war, he attempted to reach Eretz Israel aboard the immigrant ship Latrun, was interned by the British authorities in Cyprus, and later arrived in Palestine to begin a new life. In Eretz Israel he adopted the name Shalom Sechvi and later became an Israeli artist whose work was deeply connected to the memory of the Holocaust.

The Holocaust Years

Sechvi survived the Holocaust as a teenager. According to biographical sources, he was imprisoned in the Grünberg camp in Silesia and in Kittlitztreben, a subcamp of the Gross-Rosen concentration camp. He also survived a death march.

The experience of camps, forced labor, and the loss of his family became central to his life. Later, Sechvi repeatedly returned to the subject of the Holocaust in his artworks, creating pieces with autobiographical and memorial significance.

For him, art became a way to express the feelings and memories of the war years. The Israeli Film Archive notes that the need to express what he had endured during the war pushed Sechvi toward painting and artistic creation.

Journey to Eretz Israel and a New Life

After liberation, Sechvi joined the stream of Jewish survivors seeking to leave Europe and begin a new life in Eretz Israel. He sailed on the illegal immigrant ship Latrun, but was detained by the British authorities and interned in Cyprus.

After arriving in Palestine, he began his life anew at Kibbutz Afikim. He later joined the Haganah, fought in the Jordan Valley area, escorted supply convoys to Jerusalem and besieged Kfar Etzion, and was wounded during the attack on the last convoy to Etzion near Nabi Daniel.

In the years that followed, Sechvi lived and worked in Ness Ziona, and his artistic activity became an important part of his personal recovery after the catastrophe.

Artistic Style and Subject Matter

Shalom Sechvi worked in painting, sculpture, and mixed media. He studied and developed his artistic skills under the artist Stefan Alexander. His practice included oil painting, sculpture, and a distinctive collage-like technique in which he layered images over one another.

The memory of the Holocaust became one of the main themes of his work. Many of Sechvi’s pieces were autobiographical, reflecting the traumatic experience of the camps, loss, and survival.

Another important direction in his work was his engagement with Hebrew and Jewish writing. In his later works, he explored the form of letters, text, sign, and memory, connecting personal experience with the wider Jewish cultural tradition.

Collections, Exhibitions, and Legacy

The Israel Museum in Jerusalem lists Shalom Sechvi as an Israeli painter and sculptor born in Poland, confirming his place in the Israeli art archive.

His works were shown in numerous exhibitions, including a solo exhibition at the Ipanema Gallery in Tel Aviv in 1976.

In 2011, Sechvi’s autobiographical story, My Heart Beats Again, was presented at the theater in Solingen, Germany, the twin city of Ness Ziona. The production included projections of his autobiographical works, emphasizing the connection between his personal testimony and artistic legacy.

Today, Shalom Sechvi is remembered as an artist-witness: a Holocaust survivor, forced labor camp prisoner, fighter in the struggle for Israel’s creation, and creator of works in which personal memory becomes artistic testimony to destruction, survival, and spiritual renewal.
