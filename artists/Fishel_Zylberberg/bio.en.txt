🇵🇱 Poland

Fishel Zylberberg (Zber) (1909–1942)

Country of Origin: Poland

Status: Jewish Artist Murdered in the Holocaust

Biography

Fishel Zylberberg, better known by the pseudonym Zber, was born on June 23, 1909, in the city of Płock, Poland, into a traditional Jewish family. His artistic talent became evident at an early age, and with the support of the local Jewish community he was able to continue his studies in Warsaw. He later attended the Academy of Fine Arts in Warsaw, where he studied painting, graphic arts, and woodcut techniques under the renowned artist Władysław Skoczylas.

In 1936, his works were exhibited in the exhibition Black and White in Warsaw, after which he received a scholarship that enabled him to continue his artistic education in Paris. There, his talent quickly gained recognition, and critics regarded him as one of the most promising young graphic artists of his generation.

The Holocaust

After the outbreak of World War II, Zber remained in France. Together with his wife, Stenia Bonder, he was connected to anti-Nazi resistance activities. On May 14, 1941, he was arrested during a mass roundup of Jews and sent to the Beaune-la-Rolande internment camp. Even while imprisoned, he continued to draw and create portraits of fellow detainees.

In July 1942, he was transferred to the Pithiviers camp and later deported to Auschwitz in one of the first transports of Jews from France. On October 26, 1942, Fishel Zylberberg was murdered in Auschwitz at the age of only thirty-three.

Most of his artistic work was destroyed during the war. After the liberation of Paris, several dozen surviving prints were recovered, preserving the memory of a remarkable artist whose life was tragically cut short.

Artistic Style and Subject Matter

Zber is regarded as one of the most talented Jewish printmakers of his generation. He is best known for his woodcuts, characterized by powerful lines, dramatic contrasts, and deep emotional expression.

His works often depicted Jewish life in Poland, the people of his native Płock, workers, rural scenes, city streets, and landscapes. Critics praised his ability to convey both the dignity and inner strength of ordinary people.

Collections

Surviving works by Fishel Zylberberg are held in the collections of the Israel Museum in Jerusalem, the Tel Aviv Museum of Art, the Haifa Museum of Art, the National Library of France, and other museums and private collections worldwide.
