🇧🇬 Bulgaria

Dan Moskona (1931–2024)

Country of Origin: Bulgaria

Status: Jewish Refugee from Bulgaria

Biography

Dan Moskona was born in Bulgaria in 1931. In 1948, he arrived in Eretz Israel with his family and became part of the generation that helped build the young Jewish state.

After arriving in Israel, Moskona studied at the Avni Institute in Tel Aviv under renowned artists Yohanan Simon and Marcel Janco. He later continued his artistic education in Paris at the Académie de la Grande Chaumière. Over the course of many decades, he worked as a professional artist and became known for his expressive landscapes and cityscapes of Israel.

War Years and Immigration to Eretz Israel

During World War II, the Moskona family lived under the threat of persecution. According to the artist’s own recollections, during the years of German presence in Bulgaria, the family was protected by a Bulgarian police officer who was a close friend of his father and helped them avoid danger.

Later, the family left Bulgaria through Yugoslavia, which they reached by train. From there, they continued their journey aboard the ship Bonim VeLohamim (“Builders and Fighters”), one of the vessels that brought Jewish immigrants to Eretz Israel.

This journey marked the beginning of a new chapter in the artist’s life in the country that would soon become the State of Israel. The experiences of the war years, immigration, and his early life in Israel later influenced his worldview and artistic work.

Artistic Style and Subject Matter

Dan Moskona is best known for his landscapes and urban scenes of Israel. Jerusalem, Safed, Tiberias, and other historic cities occupy a special place in his artistic legacy.

His paintings are characterized by rich color, expressive light, and a deep sensitivity to the atmosphere of ancient streets and stone architecture. Moskona’s works capture the beauty of the Israeli landscape, the timeless character of its architecture, and the unique spirit of the country’s historic cities.

Collections

Works by Dan Moskona are held in private collections in Israel and abroad. His paintings have appeared regularly in exhibitions and auctions devoted to twentieth-century Israeli art.
