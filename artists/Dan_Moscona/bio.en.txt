Painting Style & Subject Matter
Moscona’s style is deeply rooted in Figurative Expressionism, characterized by a bold use of color and a structural approach to composition. His work often feels monumental, even when depicting everyday scenes, reflecting the influence of Social Realism he encountered during his early training.

His primary subjects and stylistic traits include:

The Human Form in Motion: He was fascinated by people at work and play—laborers, dancers, and musicians—often using thick, energetic brushstrokes to convey vitality and physical presence.

Old Jaffa and Mediterranean Light: Much of his work focuses on the architecture and atmosphere of Jaffa. He had a unique ability to capture the specific "golden hour" light of the Mediterranean coast.

Social Realism and Everyday Life: Influenced by Yohanan Simon, many of his paintings depict the collective life of early Israel, including kibbutz scenes and crowded city markets, treated with dignity and warmth.

Texture and Materiality: His "Oil on Canvas" works are known for their rich impasto and layered textures, which give the paintings a physical, almost sculptural quality.
